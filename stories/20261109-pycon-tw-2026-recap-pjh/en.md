title: PyCon Taiwan 2026 Recap
---

I attended PyCon Taiwan 2026.
It was my first time visiting Taiwan and my first time attending a conference abroad, so I was both nervous and excited. I was curious how it would differ from PyCon Korea, and I was looking forward to meeting many developers in a new environment.

![Checking my PyCon Taiwan 2026 name badge](/content-media/stories/20261109-pycon-tw-2026-recap-pjh/01.webp)

![A commemorative photo at the PyCon Taiwan 2026 venue](/content-media/stories/20261109-pycon-tw-2026-recap-pjh/02.webp)

When I arrived at the venue, I wrote my name on my badge and took some commemorative photos, and it finally sank in that I was really at PyCon Taiwan.

![Booths at the PyCon Taiwan 2026 venue](/content-media/stories/20261109-pycon-tw-2026-recap-pjh/03.webp)

![Booths at the PyCon Taiwan 2026 venue](/content-media/stories/20261109-pycon-tw-2026-recap-pjh/04.webp)

I visited the many booths set up at the venue and listened to explanations of the technologies and services each one introduced. I got to explore a variety of projects and services I don't usually come across, and I also had time to talk with the booth staff in person.

![The PyLadies Kaohsiung booth](/content-media/stories/20261109-pycon-tw-2026-recap-pjh/05.webp)

I also stopped by the PyLadies Kaohsiung booth and chatted with the members. Even though we're part of the same PyLadies community, it was great to learn what kinds of activities are happening in a different region. I was especially surprised that one of the members spoke Korean really well, and it was a pleasure to meet and talk in person.

![In front of the PyLadies Seoul community poster](/content-media/stories/20261109-pycon-tw-2026-recap-pjh/06.webp)

At the PyLadies Seoul poster booth, I introduced the community activities we've run so far and the conference planned for November. I explained the poster to participants who visited the booth and talked directly with people interested in PyLadies Seoul's activities.

![Claudia Ng's session](/content-media/stories/20261109-pycon-tw-2026-recap-pjh/07.webp)

I attended sessions on a variety of topics, and the one that stood out most was Claudia Ng's *Learning from 8,000+ Prompts to My AI App Without Reading the Messages* on the second day. The talk presented a case of analyzing service usage patterns with topic modeling, without directly reading the user conversations accumulated in an AI service. I found it fascinating that data could be used for analysis while still protecting personal information.

It wasn't easy to listen to technical content in a foreign language and understand it right away, so this experience reminded me once again that I need to keep studying English haha

Thanks to PyLadies Seoul, I was able to attend PyCon Taiwan, explore a variety of sessions and booths, talk with many developers, gain new insights, and have a wonderful experience.
